History / The movement

How the image was remade.

In little more than a decade, artists in Moscow, Petrograd and Vitebsk pushed painting to a black square and then out past it — into porcelain, textile, theatre and the printed page. This catalogue documents that movement, entry by entry.

1913

Rupture

Rayonism, Cubo-Futurism and the opera Victory over the Sun break Russian art from representation.

1915

Suprematism

At 0,10 in Petrograd, Malevich hangs the Black Square — non-objective painting declared.

1919–21

Into the object

At Vitebsk and INKhUK the geometry leaves the canvas for design; Chekhonin and Suetin bring it onto porcelain.

1922–24

Reach

The avant-garde spreads to textile, theatre, typography and the printed page before official taste turns against it.

The archive sets painting and applied art side by side: canvases by Malevich and Lissitzky next to Chekhonin's revolutionary porcelain and Exter's stage design. Read together, the sixteen entries show the avant-garde not as a handful of famous pictures but as a coherent effort to redesign the whole visible world.
