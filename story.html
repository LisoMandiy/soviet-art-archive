History / Edik Natanov

How the archive formed.

As documented by Edik Natanov, the collection holds sixteen works and some sixty archival photographs — and its most public chapter was a run of regional exhibitions between 2005 and 2009.

mid-1990s

The collection begins

Edik Natanov starts assembling works of the Russian avant-garde, focusing on painting, works on paper and revolutionary porcelain.

2005

Four regional exhibitions

The collection tours Yaroslavl, Nizhny Novgorod, Perm and Yekaterinburg — the 'Unknown Avant-Garde' project bringing the works to regional audiences.

2009

Yekaterinburg, again

A return show, 'Russian Avant-Garde and Its National Origins', tied to the city's international year.

2014–2026

A public catalogue

The collection's catalogue and its 60 exhibition photographs are organised into a public online archive.

The indirect contribution is the archive itself: not a single famous canvas in a capital museum, but a coherent body of works and documents — porcelain, works on paper, exhibition photographs — kept together and made legible. That is what this catalogue preserves.
